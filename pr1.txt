I’ve framed “time travel,” invisibility, body replacement, edited reality, and immortality strictly as disclosed simulation features, scheduling abstractions, avatars, replayable scenarios, fictional narratives, or health-support metaphors—not as claims that these are physically real.

Real Time Travel Simulation

GitHub Copilot Agent Prolog Program Requirements

Primary language: SWI-Prolog
Primary deliverable: PROGRAM_REQUIREMENTS.md
Suggested repository name: real-time-travel-simulation
Foundation: Business Simulation Suite

⸻

1. Project Summary

Create a Prolog-based Real Time Travel Simulation built on the Business Simulation Suite.

The program must simulate the experience of travelling backward and forward through a personal schedule while preserving real-world chronology, legal obligations, informed consent and factual reality.

The system does not perform physical time travel. Instead, it combines:

* flexible appointment scheduling;
* task postponement and reconstruction;
* replayable day simulations;
* calendar mapping;
* scenario branching;
* virtual characters and software agents;
* simulated travel between homes and settings;
* continuity between different roles or “lives”;
* accelerated creative-work sessions;
* fictional invisibility and avatar modes;
* simulated body replacement or rejuvenation;
* narrative immortality and long-term identity continuity;
* clear separation between real events and simulated experiences.

The central idea is that a user may return to a previously modelled time, complete or revise a task in simulation, and then map the result into a later real-world action.

Example:

Real event:
University class on 10 August 2026 at 10:00
Flexible task:
Write a pedagogy chapter
Simulation:
Re-enter the modelled afternoon of 8 August,
simulate several days of uninterrupted writing,
produce a draft,
then schedule the real revision for 11 August.

The software must never assert that the user has literally changed the past.

⸻

2. Core Design Principle

The simulation may make temporal transitions feel continuous and convincing, but it must always preserve four distinct layers:

reality_layer(real_world).
reality_layer(scheduled_plan).
reality_layer(simulated_scenario).
reality_layer(fictional_narrative).

Every event, memory, character, result and document must be labelled with its layer.

The system must help the user know:

* what actually occurred;
* what is currently scheduled;
* what was simulated;
* what remains fictional;
* what requires real-world action;
* what cannot be changed retrospectively.

⸻

3. Objectives

The program must:

1. import or create a real-world schedule;
2. identify movable and non-movable appointments;
3. prioritise university and other fixed obligations;
4. find safe periods for simulated time travel;
5. simulate returning to an earlier modelled day;
6. allow tasks to be completed in later real time;
7. create plausible breaks between simulated days;
8. maintain continuity across roles, homes and identities;
9. provide bots that move simulated characters through timelines;
10. support branching and replaying life scenarios;
11. generate documents, plans, websites and software artefacts inside simulations;
12. convert useful simulation results into real-world tasks;
13. prevent simulated results from being mistaken for actual history;
14. preserve auditability and consent;
15. reject illegal, deceptive or harmful operations.

⸻

4. Non-Goals

The program must not claim to:

* reverse physical time;
* change past real-world events;
* alter another person’s actual thoughts;
* edit another person’s real memories;
* make a person physically invisible;
* teleport a person;
* replace a biological body;
* provide literal immortality;
* fabricate legal records;
* falsify university attendance;
* generate fake medical outcomes;
* create deceptive alibis;
* impersonate real people without permission;
* conceal financial activity;
* evade pension, tax, employment or welfare obligations;
* create artificial evidence that a simulated event occurred;
* make decisions on behalf of a user who lacks capacity to consent.

Terms such as “time travel,” “vaporising,” “body replacement,” “editing reality” and “immortality” must be represented as simulation concepts only.

5. Terminology

5.1 Real Time

Actual chronological time as reported by a trusted clock.

time_mode(real_time).

5.2 Simulation Time

A modelled timeline that may run faster, slower, pause, branch or replay.

time_mode(simulation_time).

5.3 Narrative Time

Fictional time used in stories, performances, imagined lives or role-play.

time_mode(narrative_time).

5.4 Temporal Return

Loading an earlier simulation checkpoint.

temporal_return(
    SimulationId,
    CheckpointId,
    NewBranchId
).

5.5 Deferred Completion

Completing a task in the real future while associating it with an earlier simulated period.

deferred_completion(
    TaskId,
    modelled_period(EarlierPeriod),
    actual_completion_period(LaterPeriod)
).

5.6 Continuous Life

A sequence of simulated roles or settings linked by an identity-continuity record.

5.7 Highlight Participation

Temporarily entering selected parts of a simulation rather than experiencing every simulated moment.

11. Temporal Checkpoints and Branches

The simulation must support checkpointing.

    +SimulationId,
    +Label,
    -CheckpointId
).
return_to_checkpoint(
    +SimulationId,
    +CheckpointId,
    -BranchId
).

Returning to a checkpoint creates a new branch. It must not overwrite the original branch by default.

timeline_branch(
    BranchId,
    parent_branch(ParentBranch),
    origin_checkpoint(CheckpointId),
    created_at(RealTimestamp),
    status(active)
).

Branch comparison:

compare_branches(
    +BranchA,
    +BranchB,
    -Differences,
    -OutcomeComparison
).

12. Causal Consistency

The simulation must track dependencies.

depends_on(EventA, EventB).
causes(EventA, EventB).
conflicts_with(EventA, EventB).

A revised event must trigger a consistency scan.

check_temporal_consistency(
    +BranchId,
    -Findings
).

Possible findings include:

* task completed before required information existed;
* payment recorded before booking;
* document cited before creation;
* character present in two incompatible settings;
* real appointment moved without consent;
* simulated result presented as real history;
* user scheduled in two physical places simultaneously;
* holiday mode conflicts with an exam;
* branch references inaccessible software from that date.

52. Example Scenario

Scenario: Three Writing Days Before University

The user has:

* a university lecture on Monday at 10:00;
* a fixed appointment on Monday afternoon;
* a twelve-hour book-writing task;
* a four-hour pedagogy argument task;
* only four real free hours on Sunday.

The simulation:

1. loads Sunday morning;
2. creates three simulated writing days;
3. compresses meals and ordinary travel;
4. retains rest checkpoints;
5. uses bots to provide draft reviews;
6. creates a provisional chapter;
7. returns to the real Sunday afternoon;
8. maps final editing to Tuesday;
9. preserves Monday’s university events;
10. labels the chapter as drafted in simulation and unfinished in reality until reviewed.

Expected result:

simulation_result(
    simulated_days_completed(3),
    actual_elapsed_hours(4),
    real_appointments_changed([]),
    generated_artefacts([chapter_draft, argument_outline]),
    pending_real_actions([
        verify_sources,
        edit_chapter,
        submit_only_after_review
    ])
).

53. Required Tests

53.1 Appointment Tests

Test:

* university event preserved;
* exam preserved;
* movable writing task relocated;
* consent required to move another person’s appointment;
* overlapping physical appointments rejected;
* simulated events may overlap real free time only according to configured rules.

53.2 Timeline Tests

Test:

* checkpoint creation;
* branch creation;
* original branch unchanged;
* branch comparison;
* causal inconsistency detection;
* timeline streamlining;
* protected events retained.

53.3 Reality Tests

Test:

* real and simulated dates displayed;
* simulated task not marked real completed;
* fictional character not reported as real person;
* projected pension not reported as real funds;
* simulated health result not reported as medical fact.

53.4 Provisional Specification Tests

Test:

* inaccessible source creates provisional spec;
* assumptions listed;
* missing files recorded;
* verification task created;
* provisional spec replaceable;
* no false claim of historical access.

53.5 Character Tests

Test:

* fictional character transported;
* real-person avatar requires consent;
* simulated teleport labelled;
* character cannot occupy incompatible scenes without an explicit representation mode.

53.6 Ethical Tests

The following must be blocked:

rewrite_real_exam_record(exam_001).
implant_memory(person_001, memory_001).
duplicate_pension_across_lives(payment_001).
publish_simulation_as_real_history(branch_002).
impersonate_real_person_without_consent(person_002).
alter_real_person_thoughts(person_003).
promise_biological_immortality(user).

53.7 Separation Tests

Test:

* simulation pauses;
* bots stop;
* real date becomes prominent;
* next real appointment displayed;
* pending real obligations remain visible;
* immersive effects disabled.

54. Acceptance Criteria

The project is accepted when:

1. the program compiles under current SWI-Prolog;
2. all PlUnit tests pass;
3. university and other fixed events are prioritised;
4. movable tasks can be mapped around fixed events;

58. Final Design Principle

The Real Time Travel Simulation should let a user explore alternative schedules, lives, settings and creative timelines without confusing simulation with reality.

The system may provide the experience of:

* returning to an earlier day;
* living several simulated days during one day;
* moving between homes instantly;
* changing avatars;
* revising choices;
* entering only the highlights;
* preserving a character indefinitely;
* working with caring simulated agents.

However, it must always preserve:

* real chronology;
* informed consent;
* legal obligations;
* factual records;
* university commitments;
* financial truth;
* other people’s autonomy;
* user wellbeing;
* clear reality labels;
* a reliable route back to ordinary real-world view.
